# Maritime zones in Indian waters — what each one means

Source material for ORCA's retrieval layer. Explains what a boundary *is* and what
crossing it means. Contains **no forecast values** — numbers reach the user only as
`evidence[]` from an adapter, never from retrieval, because a retrieved number has no
timestamp and no provenance.

---

## International Maritime Boundary Line (IMBL)

**What it is.** The agreed sea border between India and a neighbouring state, fixed by
treaty, by a median-line agreement, or by an international court ruling. India has IMBLs
with Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Indonesia and the Maldives.

**What crossing it means.** Fishing across the IMBL without authorisation is the single
most common reason Indian fishing boats are detained. The neighbouring coast guard may
arrest the crew and impound the vessel. Boats have been held for months. This is a legal
and personal-safety matter, not an administrative one.

**Where it bites hardest.** The Palk Strait and Gulf of Mannar, between India and Sri
Lanka, are narrow and heavily fished from both sides. The boundary can be a few kilometres
from productive ground, and GPS drift alone can put a boat across it.

**What to do.** Turn back toward Indian waters as soon as you are warned. A warning that
arrives after the crossing is worthless — that is why ORCA alerts on approach.

---

## Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)

**What it is.** The sea area extending 200 nautical miles from India's baseline, where
India holds sovereign rights over fishing and other resources. India's EEZ is about
2.3 million km² across three separate bodies: the mainland, the Andaman & Nicobar Islands
and Lakshadweep.

**What being inside it means.** For an Indian vessel, being inside India's EEZ is the
normal, lawful state. It is not a restriction and not a warning.

**What being outside it means.** Outside the EEZ you may be on the high seas, which any
vessel may lawfully use, or inside another state's EEZ, which you may not fish in without
a licence. Leaving India's EEZ is not by itself an offence — crossing an IMBL into a
neighbour's waters is.

---

## Marine Protected Area (MPA)

**What it is.** A designated conservation zone — a marine national park, sanctuary or
biosphere reserve — protecting reefs, mangroves, turtle nesting grounds or seagrass beds.
Indian examples include the Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park, the Gulf of Kachchh
Marine National Park and the Sundarbans.

**What entering it means.** Fishing inside an MPA is restricted or prohibited depending on
its designation. Penalties fall under the Wildlife Protection Act and state fisheries
rules, and can include gear confiscation and prosecution.

**Why it matters beyond the law.** MPAs are usually breeding and nursery grounds. Fishing
them out reduces next season's catch in the surrounding waters too.

---

## Territorial sea and the straight baseline

**Baseline.** The line along the coast from which all other maritime zones are measured.
It is a reference line, **not a boundary** — being near it carries no consequence. ORCA
deliberately excludes baselines from proximity alerts, because they hug the shore and
would otherwise fire a warning at every harbour.

**Territorial sea.** 12 nautical miles from the baseline, under full Indian sovereignty.

**Contiguous zone.** 24 nautical miles, where India may enforce customs, immigration and
sanitary law.

---

## Fishing ban period

India enforces an annual monsoon fishing ban to protect spawning stock — roughly
15 April to 14 June on the east coast and 1 June to 31 July on the west coast, with the
exact dates set by each state each year. Mechanised vessels are prohibited from fishing
during the ban.

INCOIS does not issue Potential Fishing Zone advisories during the ban period. **No
advisory is a normal state, not a failure** — a system that cannot tell "none issued"
from "we could not fetch" is misleading its user.
